**Last updated:** 2025-11-10  
**Context:** Nobel Prize in Physics 2025 awarded for pioneering work on Josephson junctions and superconducting quantum circuits

## The Nobel 2025 Breakthrough

The 2025 Nobel Prize in Physics recognized groundbreaking research demonstrating that **macroscopic circuits** (millions of atoms) can exhibit genuine **quantum behavior**. Josephson junctions—interfaces between superconducting materials—enable electrons to tunnel coherently across barriers, creating artificial atoms with controllable quantum states.

**Key insight:** Quantum mechanics isn't confined to individual particles. Carefully engineered macroscopic systems can sustain quantum coherence, opening pathways to quantum computing, precision sensing, and fundamental physics exploration.

**Technical achievement:**
- **Superconducting qubits** (transmons, flux qubits) operate near absolute zero (~10-50 mK)
- **Coherence times** reached milliseconds (T2 ~100 µs typical)
- **Control fidelity** exceeds 99.9% for single-qubit gates

## From Cryogenic Circuits to Biological Platforms

### The Conceptual Bridge

The Nobel work establishes a crucial precedent: **artificial quantum systems** can be designed, controlled, and applied far beyond natural atomic scales. This atlas extends that exploration into an orthogonal frontier:

**Superconducting circuits** → **Biological quantum sensors**

| Dimension | Josephson Systems | Biological Qubits Atlas |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------------|
| **Operating temperature** | 10-50 mK (cryogenic) | 270-320 K (ambient/physiological) |
| **Architecture** | Engineered metal junctions | Protein complexes, defect centers, molecular spins |
| **Applications** | Quantum computing, metrology | Biological sensing, imaging, hypothesized quantum biology |
| **Coherence regime** | Isolated, noise-shielded | Functional under biological noise (noise-assisted processes) |

### Why "Post-Josephson, Room-Temperature Platforms"?

The Nobel demonstrates that **macroscopic quantum engineering is viable**. This atlas catalogues systems that pursue similar goals without cryogenic infrastructure:

1. **NV centers in nanodiamonds** — ODMR-controlled spin qubits operating at 295 K
2. **Fluorescent protein biosensors** — Optical reporters with quantum optical properties (FRET, superresolution)
3. **Hyperpolarized nuclear spins** — ¹³C, ¹⁵N agents for metabolic imaging in vivo
4. **Radical pair mechanisms** — Hypothesized quantum effects in cryptochrome proteins (magnetoreception)

**Critical distinction:** These systems don't compete with superconducting qubits for computational supremacy. They address **orthogonal applications** where ambient operation, biocompatibility, and in vivo deployment are paramount.

## The Role of This Atlas

### Positioning as "Room-Temperature Quantum Platforms"

This atlas is **not** a repository of quantum computers. It is a curated database of systems that:

✅ Operate at or near physiological temperatures (270-320 K)  
✅ Exhibit measurable quantum properties (coherence, entanglement, spin control)  
✅ Are compatible with biological contexts (in cellulo, in vivo)  
✅ Enable sensing, imaging, or hypothesized quantum-assisted biological functions

**Version v2.2.2** provides:
- **180 curated systems** (Tier 1: modeling-ready, full provenance)
- **13 candidate systems** (Tier 2: incomplete metadata, curation queue)
- **103 staging systems** (Tier 3: auto-harvested, transparency audit)

### Connection to Nobel Legacy

The Josephson junction breakthrough opened quantum engineering as a discipline. This atlas explores an adjacent possibility space:

> **"If macroscopic circuits can be quantum, can biological macromolecules also leverage quantum effects for function?"**

The atlas does not claim to answer this question definitively. It **documents existing experimental evidence**:
- NV-based thermometry in neurons (ODMR readout)
- Hyperpolarized ¹³C pyruvate tracking glycolysis in tumors (FDA-approved)
- Voltage sensors with Förster resonance energy transfer (quantum dipole coupling)

**Scope limitation:** Systems labeled "Class D" (mechanistic candidates) represent hypotheses under active debate. This atlas maintains strict provenance (DOI required) and explicit confidence grading.
